Sending the same CV to every job is the fastest way to apply and the slowest way to get interviews. Tailoring does not mean rewriting your CV from scratch each time. With a good base version and a short routine, you can adapt it to a new job in 15 to 20 minutes.

## Start with a master CV

Keep one longer document that contains everything: every role, project, achievement, skill, course and certification, with numbers wherever you have them. You will never send this version, but it means you are always choosing from complete material instead of trying to remember what you did.

## The 20-minute tailoring routine

**1. Pull out the keywords (5 minutes).** Read the job description and list the required skills, tools, qualifications and the exact job title. Mark which are must-haves.

**2. Rewrite the summary (5 minutes).** Use the job title, your years of experience and the two or three most important skills from your list.

**3. Reorder and reword your experience (5–8 minutes).**
- Move the most relevant bullet points to the top of each role.
- Reword bullets to use the employer's terms where your experience genuinely matches.
- Cut bullets that have nothing to do with this role, especially for older jobs.

**4. Adjust the skills section (2 minutes).** Put the job's key skills first, spelled the way the advert spells them.

## What not to change

- **Facts.** Employers, job titles, dates and qualifications stay exactly as they are. Recruiters and background checks will compare them.
- **Skills you do not have.** Adding them may get you past a filter, but it will not get you through the interview and can cost you an offer later.

## How to know it worked

Compare your tailored CV against the job description before you send it:

- Are all the required skills you genuinely have mentioned?
- Do they appear in your experience, not only in a skills list?
- Is the job title from the advert in your headline or summary?
- Is everything still true?
