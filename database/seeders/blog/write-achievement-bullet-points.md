Most CVs describe what someone was *responsible for*. The best ones show what they *achieved*. That difference is what makes a recruiter stop skimming, and it is also where your keywords carry the most weight with an applicant tracking system.

## Duties versus achievements

**Duty:**
> Responsible for managing social media accounts.

**Achievement:**
> Grew Instagram followers from 4,000 to 25,000 in 12 months by launching a weekly video series, increasing website referrals by 35%.

Both describe the same job. Only one tells the reader how well you did it.

## A formula that works: action + task + result

Build each bullet from three parts:

1. **Start with a strong action verb:** built, led, reduced, launched, negotiated, automated, designed.
2. **Say what you did, using the job's keywords** where they genuinely apply.
3. **End with the result**, measured if you can: time saved, money earned, errors reduced, people trained, customers served.

> **Automated** monthly sales reporting **in Excel and Power BI**, **cutting preparation time from two days to two hours.**

## Where to find numbers

Many people think their job has no metrics. Almost every role does:

- **Volume:** customers handled, tickets closed, products shipped, students taught
- **Speed:** turnaround times, response times, deadlines met
- **Money:** budget managed, revenue generated, costs reduced
- **Quality:** error rates, satisfaction scores, audit results
- **Scale:** team size, number of locations, users of a system

If you do not have exact figures, honest estimates with words like "about" or "over" are acceptable, as long as you can explain them in an interview.

## Words to replace

| Instead of | Try |
| --- | --- |
| Responsible for | Led, managed, owned |
| Helped with | Contributed to, supported, delivered |
| Worked on | Built, developed, designed |
| Was involved in | Coordinated, implemented, launched |

## How many bullets per role?

For recent and relevant roles, three to six bullets is usually right. For older or less relevant roles, one or two lines are enough. Put your strongest, most relevant achievement first in each role, because many readers only see the first line.

## Make every bullet do two jobs

A strong bullet persuades the recruiter and contains a keyword the ATS is looking for. "Reduced page load time by 40% by optimising MySQL queries and adding Redis caching" proves impact *and* covers three technical keywords.
