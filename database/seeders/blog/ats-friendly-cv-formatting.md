A beautifully designed CV can still fail if an applicant tracking system cannot read it. The parser has to turn your document into fields such as name, job titles, dates and skills. When the layout confuses it, information ends up in the wrong place or disappears. These formatting rules keep your CV readable by both software and people.

## Use a single-column layout

Two-column designs look modern, but many parsers read straight across the page, mixing your sidebar into your work history. A single column guarantees the reading order is the one you intended.

## Avoid tables, text boxes and graphics

- **Tables** are often read cell by cell in an unexpected order, or skipped.
- **Text boxes and shapes** may be ignored completely.
- **Icons, logos, photos and skill bars** carry no readable text. "Python ●●●●○" tells a parser nothing.

If information matters, it should be plain text in the main body of the document.

## Keep contact details in the body, not the header

Some systems skip the page header and footer. Put your name, phone number, email address, city and LinkedIn URL at the top of the page as normal text.

## Use standard section headings

Parsers look for familiar headings to decide what each part of your CV is. Stick to:

- Summary (or Profile)
- Experience (or Work Experience)
- Skills
- Education
- Certifications, Projects, Languages (if relevant)

"My Journey" or "Where I've Been" may look friendly, but a parser may not recognise them as your experience.

## Choose common fonts and simple formatting

Use widely available fonts such as Arial, Calibri, Helvetica, Georgia or Times New Roman, in 10–12 pt for body text. Bold and italics are fine. Avoid unusual symbols as bullet points; standard round bullets or hyphens are safest.

## Write dates consistently

Pick one format and use it everywhere, for example "Jan 2022 – Present" or "01/2022 – 06/2024". Consistent dates help the system calculate your years of experience correctly.

## Save in the right file type

A text-based PDF or a Word document (.docx) works with almost every modern ATS. Two things to check:

- If you can select and copy the text in your PDF, it is text-based. A scanned image of a CV cannot be read.
- If the employer specifically asks for .docx, send .docx.

## A quick self-test

Copy all the text from your CV and paste it into a plain text editor. If the result is in a sensible order, with your contact details, job titles, dates and skills all present, a parser will most likely read it well. If sections are jumbled or missing, simplify the layout.
