The summary at the top of your CV is the first thing a recruiter reads and one of the first places an applicant tracking system looks for keywords. In three or four sentences it should answer one question: *why are you a strong fit for this specific job?*

## What a good summary does

- **States who you are in the employer's terms**, ideally using the job title from the advert.
- **Shows your level**, usually through years of experience or scope of responsibility.
- **Names the most important skills** the job asks for that you genuinely have.
- **Ends with evidence**, such as a result, a specialism or the kind of value you bring.

## A simple structure

1. **Title and experience:** "Laravel Developer with 4 years of experience building web applications for SaaS and e-commerce companies."
2. **Core skills that match the job:** "Specialises in REST APIs, MySQL performance tuning and automated testing with PHPUnit."
3. **Proof or focus:** "Recently cut average API response times by 40% and led the migration of a monolith to queued, event-driven services."

That is three sentences, and every one of them is specific.

## Before and after

**Before:**
> Hard-working and passionate professional seeking a challenging role where I can use my skills and grow with a dynamic company.

This says nothing a recruiter can use, and it contains no searchable skills.

**After:**
> Customer Success Manager with 5 years in B2B SaaS, managing a portfolio of 60+ accounts worth $2M in annual revenue. Experienced in onboarding, renewals and Salesforce reporting. Raised net revenue retention from 96% to 108% in 18 months.

It names the role, the industry, the scale, the tools and a measurable result.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Writing an objective instead of a summary.** Employers care about what you offer, not what you are seeking.
- **Using "I" and "me".** Summaries are traditionally written without first-person pronouns.
- **Listing soft skills with no proof.** "Excellent communicator" is weak; "presented quarterly results to the leadership team" shows it.
- **Using the same summary for every job.** The summary is the easiest part of your CV to tailor, and the one with the most impact.

## Tailor it in two minutes

For each application, swap in the exact job title from the advert and the two or three skills the job emphasises most, as long as they are true for you. Keep the structure; change the words.
